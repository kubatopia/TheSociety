The courthouse was built in 1824, significantly expanded in 1929, and retired from
county use in 1996 — the year the Martinsville-Henry County Historical Society was
formed to preserve it.

## What's inside

The former courthouse holds exhibits on local history, including:

- Means of communication — print, sound and television
- Home sewing, and the textile industry that followed it
- The tobacco heritage
- The furniture industry that created great wealth in the area
- Local musicians and area nightlife
- Military service

## The annex

A 13,000-square-foot annex sits behind the museum, connected to the former
courthouse by a skywalk. It houses the King Collections: Native American artifacts,
trains, tools, a gambling parlor, firearms, ivory, early machines, baskets and
pottery, dollhouses, medical devices, steam whistles, locks, horse tack and a great
deal more.

## Group visits and classes

The museum offers tours to classes and community groups, and Society specialists
give talks on a range of local topics. Get in touch to arrange one.

## Renting the building

The stately structure is available for rent for weddings, meetings, reunions,
shows and more. Contact the Society for rates and availability.
